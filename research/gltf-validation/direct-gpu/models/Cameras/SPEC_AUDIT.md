Camera baseline: [published glTF 2.0.1](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html), registry commit `8e798b02d254cea97659a333cfcb20875b62bdd4`.

Sections 3.10.1–3.10.3 define camera transforms and projection matrices without an additional mandatory FOV upper bound. Section 5.14.2 recommends `yfov < pi`; sections 5.13.1–5.13.2 recommend nonnegative magnitudes but prohibit zero. Signed magnitudes and larger positive FOV therefore remain applicable, discouraged inputs. Parser acceptance and actual imported-camera behavior are separate gates.

Published glTF 2.0.1 §3.10.2 derives the camera view from its node's global transform with scale ignored. A reflected world matrix has no unique proper rotation after discarding signed scales, and a sheared world matrix has no exact scale-and-rotation decomposition. A valid local TRS hierarchy can produce either world matrix. The camera view ambiguity is documented in [Khronos issue #2468](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/issues/2468).

The [current Khronos draft §3.10.2](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/specification/2.0/Specification.adoc#view-matrix) clarifies that the camera view is defined only when all three global basis axes are nonzero and the determinant after independently normalizing those axes is close to positive one. This permits nonuniform scale when the global axes are orthogonal with positive orientation. It leaves reflection and appreciable shear undefined. The draft is tracked separately from the published conformance baseline. The frozen transactional regressions establish whether a valid model resource survives an unsupported camera-view import. They assign no expected image or projection to the ambiguous views. Folding the full inverse affine transform into a projection would render a chosen policy for these cases, including scale effects that the published camera section asks clients to ignore.

The separate near-π native probe exercised the installed custom projection path before the public importer and persistent flips. An ordinary 70° camera with a 45° projection override reproduced the ordinary 45° capture byte for byte. The amplified near-π component point query then returned the same value with and without the override, with a normalized X error of 0.06392 against literal cotangent arithmetic. The [model result](result.json) records the loaded producer, capture and receipt hashes. The probe's finite raster control and near-π point-query failure are separate observations from public import and near-π raster coverage.
